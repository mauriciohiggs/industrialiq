# First 100 active users

## Target

Reach 100 Weekly Active Users who each complete at least one meaningful learning action: answer a question, review a flashcard, or submit a practice exam. Prioritize useful feedback from industrial practitioners over traffic without learning activity.

## Audience

- Technicians and engineers learning Ignition 8.1.
- Learners revisiting industrial automation concepts.
- Experienced practitioners willing to review question quality.
- Open-source contributors and subject-matter experts.

## Four-week operating loop

1. Publish short, factual updates in industrial automation communities that permit project posts. Link directly to the live app and identify it as an independent open-source project.
2. Invite users to complete one drill, then use an in-app feedback link for the exact problem they encountered.
3. Ask contributors and subject-matter experts to review one topic module rather than the entire bank.
4. Triage feedback weekly into question corrections, usability fixes, content gaps, and future requests.
5. Share a weekly changelog showing what changed because of learner feedback.

## Acquisition checklist

- Share the beta post with a direct app link and a clear one-session ask: finish a 10-question drill.
- Publish the Ignition Core track in the maintainer's professional network without making certification-pass claims.
- Invite five subject-matter experts to review one module each.
- Ask every accepted correction or contribution to be documented publicly.
- Re-engage existing testers weekly with the latest question-bank and usability improvements.

## Feedback loop

Tag every issue by type and priority. Treat reports with cited evidence as the highest priority for question quality. Dismiss no criticism merely because it is outside the roadmap; record its category and rationale.

The community page routes users to:

- User feedback
- Bug reports
- Feature requests
- Course requests
- Question reports

## KPIs and weekly targets

| KPI | Early target | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weekly Active Users | 0 to 100 over four to eight weeks | Confirms repeated learning use |
| Question Completion Rate | Establish baseline, then improve | Reveals friction in each study mode |
| Flashcard Retention | Establish baseline after users have a return interval | Shows whether repetition is useful |
| Practice Exam Completion | Establish baseline, investigate large abandonment by question count | Shows whether exam length is practical |
| User Return Rate | Establish a week-two baseline, then improve | Separates a launch spike from ongoing value |
| Feedback submissions per 100 active users | At least 5 useful submissions | Provides evidence for what to fix next |

## Weekly decision review

Review in this order:

1. Broken or unsafe content.
2. Reproducible bugs that prevent study completion.
3. Question corrections with evidence.
4. Friction seen in analytics.
5. Repeated requests for content or features.
6. Planned roadmap items.

## Success at 100 users

- At least 100 Weekly Active Users are observed.
- At least five useful feedback submissions are triaged.
- Every confirmed critical bug and evidence-backed question correction has an owner or resolution.
- The next roadmap priority is supported by behavior or repeated requests, not assumptions.
- No enterprise, account, or AI scope is added merely to accelerate acquisition.
